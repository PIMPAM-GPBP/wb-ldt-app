# Darkhorse Analytics and public data publishers: how they explain composite indices and place-level results

Research date: 2026-10-29. Scope: patterns for turning one unit's composite scores into a brief a planner can act on, as models for the LDT planning brief (806 Nepal municipalities, 161 Serbia, 116 Zambia; three pillars built from indicators; five LLM stages: evidence narrative, plan alignment, web context, SWOT, investment recommendations).

Access note: several primary pages are JavaScript-rendered or returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches (Darkhorse homepage on the old domain, countyhealthrankings.org county pages and how-to pages, the OECD July 2026 blog, hdr.undp.org, datacommons.org, globaldatalab.org profiles). Where that happened I relied on the tool's methodology or tutorial pages, the maker's own case studies, or search-result snippets, and I flag which is which. Anything older than 2023 is marked "(pre-2023)".

## Key question 1: Which Darkhorse Analytics projects present per-place profiles, rankings or narratives, and how do they explain drivers, compare peers, lead to action, and use AI?

### Takeaway
Darkhorse (now trading as Darkhorse Visualization, Edmonton) has at least seven live index or profile tools; the U.S. Climate Vulnerability Index (CVI) is the only one with a documented per-place "report" that names top drivers and shows a hierarchical composition. None of the Darkhorse projects or case studies mention AI or generated narrative; their stated approach is a drillable hierarchy from headline rank to indicator, plus printable or linkable reports, not prose.

### Cited Findings
- Darkhorse's stated philosophy: "Getting the right answer is half the battle. Convincing someone to act on it is the other half." and "We design tools that build trust by making exploration simple and the underlying data easy to understand." No mention of AI or generated text anywhere on the solutions page. [Darkhorse Visualization home](https://darkhorsevisualization.com/); [Solutions](https://darkhorsevisualization.com/solutions)
- Portfolio relevant to place scoring, per Darkhorse's own solutions page (client, year): U.S. Climate Vulnerability Index (EDF, tract ranking on 184 indicators); Opportunity Atlas and Global Opportunity Atlas (Opportunity Insights, 2025 for the global one); CORE Score (Yale and American Academy of Arts and Sciences, 2023, county wellbeing across health, economy, environment, civic life); Global Capitalism Index (UVA, 2026, 170 countries, eight subindices, country profiles and comparisons, 2009 to 2025); AIDSVu and HepVu (Emory, "Location Profiles"); Cancer Prevention Community Profiles (Alberta Health Services, 2021, "printable community insights"); ABMI ORB area-level biodiversity reporting; PHOENIX Prevalence Profiler (Wayne State); VaxImpactMap (Emory, 2026, scenario projections with state profiles); COVID-19 Vaccine Coverage Index (Surgo, 2020, "helps leaders identify vulnerable areas"). [Solutions](https://darkhorsevisualization.com/solutions)
- Darkhorse projects that were search-suggested but not confirmed on their site: none; the search finding that the CORE Score is credited to Yale's APEX at ISPS with the American Academy is confirmed by the CORE Score site itself. [corescore.us](https://corescore.us/)
- CVI design intent, per Darkhorse design lead Rob Korzan and VP Craig Hiltz (Oct 2023): indicators are browsed as a tree split between baseline vulnerabilities and climate risks; the tree structure was "the project's biggest design challenge"; audience needs (communities seeking federal funding, federal policymakers targeting communities) decided which visuals sit at the top level and the team removed "noise"; once users find the data they want, the tool generates a report. EDF's Grace Tee Lewis: similar overall scores can mask very different vulnerability profiles, which the indicator breakdown is meant to reveal. The article does not mention AI. [Taproot Edmonton, 19 Oct 2023](https://edmonton.taproot.news/news/2023/10/19/darkhorse-analytics-visualizes-us-climate-vulnerability)
- CVI per-place report structure (primary tutorial): select a tract or county, click "View Report"; the left panel lets users "understand more about specific drivers of vulnerability" and "alter the geographic context"; the right side shows scores, rankings and "a breakdown of what contributes to the overall vulnerability score". Named sections: "Indicator Spotlight" (indicator value and rank versus other locations at the same scale; "The higher the national percentile, the higher the vulnerability rating"), "Top Drivers of Overall Climate Vulnerability" (each category links to a detailed breakdown), "Composition" (per category: rank, indicator definition, vulnerability rating, national percentile), and an "Indicator Tree". Context toggles: state versus EPA region, county versus tract. Export is "Print" and "Copy Link", no data download on the report page. Top Drivers and Composition are not available for every indicator. The tutorial gives no guidance on what action to take. [CVI tutorial, step 3](https://climatevulnerabilityindex.org/tutorials/step3)
- CVI methodology: 184 indicators grouped into 37 components and 7 categories; adapts the ToxPI weighted-average approach; everything "equally weighted at each aggregation level"; raw values converted to percentiles, some inverted so higher always means more vulnerable; display scale is a 0 to 100 percentile. Missing data: small gaps filled with the median of the next larger geography; no indicator has more than 25 percent imputed and all but ten have under 5 percent; where only county or state data exist every tract in that area gets the same value; nine datasets lack Alaska and Hawaii and those are left missing without affecting the score. Most annual data are 2017 to 2019 to avoid pandemic anomalies; 2010 TIGER boundaries. Stated purpose: show where "targeted benefits and resources should be directed"; community leaders helped choose indicators. [CVI methodology](https://climatevulnerabilityindex.org/methodology/)
- CORE Score (corescore.us): 11 stated indicators (12 listed) across Economic Security, Economic Opportunity, Health, Political Voice; a county map, a time-series chart of all U.S. counties by year with the 5th to 95th percentile band marked, "Compare Arrows" to "Explore change over time", and breakdowns by age, education, income, race/ethnicity and sex. Benchmarks are the national figure and the county percentile band; no peer group, no driver explanation, no narrative, no action guidance on the page; links to a Methodology PDF and the Commission's "Final Report". In the fetched view every value read "Data is unavailable" with no explanation of how missing data is handled. [corescore.us](https://corescore.us/); Darkhorse's framing is that it helps local leaders "compare performance and prioritize action" and it won a Silver Anthem Award (post dated 20 Nov 2024). [Darkhorse Anthem post](https://darkhorsevisualization.com/blog/darkhorse-analytics-wins-two-anthem-awards-for-data-visualization-excellence)
- Global Capitalism Index case study (Darkhorse, dated 8 Oct 2026): goal was to make the research "accessible" to policymakers, business leaders, journalists, researchers and the public while keeping "the detail needed to investigate the findings"; each country profile pairs the headline score and world rank with a breakdown of all eight subindices; users compare countries directly and replay a timeline back to 2009; methodology is linked but the case study does not say how uncertainty or missing data are shown; no AI, generated text or reports mentioned. [Making Capitalism Measurable](https://darkhorsevisualization.com/blog/making-capitalism-measurable)
- GCI itself (UVA Democracy and Capitalism Lab): weights are derived by principal components analysis "without subjective weightings"; the 2025 annual report ranks 175 countries 2009 to 2025 (news coverage says 161; counts conflict across sources). [GCI whitepaper](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/DemocracyandCapitalismLab/GlobalCapitalismIndex@main/docs/gci_whitepaper.pdf); [GCI annual report 2025](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/DemocracyandCapitalismLab/GlobalCapitalismIndex@main/docs/gci_annual_report_2025.pdf)
- Opportunity Atlas (Darkhorse build for Opportunity Insights; Taproot reports about 1.3 million users after five years): estimates are for children born 1978 to 1983, so they describe past conditions; each tract estimate uses only that tract's data (average 4,250 people); the authors warn the lesson "is not necessarily that moving is the best solution" (pre-2023 research, tool still live). [Opportunity Insights atlas summary](https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/atlas_summary.pdf); [Taproot](https://edmonton.taproot.news/news/2023/10/19/darkhorse-analytics-visualizes-us-climate-vulnerability)
- Darkhorse blog index (12 posts, 2016 to 2026): no post on uncertainty, missing data or AI; two on indices (GCI case study, CORE Score award). [Darkhorse blog](https://darkhorsevisualization.com/blog)

### Inferences
- The one Darkhorse pattern worth copying directly is the CVI report's section order: overall score and rank, then "Top Drivers", then "Composition" per category with definition, rating and percentile, then a spotlight on any one indicator with its rank among peers at the same scale. That is a deterministic, data-driven skeleton that an LLM narrative could sit on top of rather than replace. It fits LDT well because LDT's three pillars from indicators are the same hierarchy at smaller depth.
- Darkhorse never lets prose carry the explanation; drivers are shown as ranked lists and composition charts. For LDT, this argues for computing the driver ranking (which indicators pull a pillar below its peer median) in code and handing the LLM only the ranked facts to narrate.
- The CVI "alter the geographic context" toggle (state versus region, county versus tract) is the closest analogue to LDT's need to compare a municipality to its province or district peers versus the national distribution. Fit: strong for Nepal (province and district tiers) and Serbia (region and district), weaker for Zambia if only province exists.
- CORE Score's percentile band on the time series (5th to 95th of all counties) is a cheap, honest way to show "where this place sits" without a peer group. Fit: good as the default LDT comparison when peer groups are not defined.
- The Opportunity Atlas warnings (historical cohort, single-tract estimates) show that even flagship tools put interpretation caveats beside the number. LDT should surface indicator vintage per pillar in the same way.

### Gaps
- The task described the CORE Score as "with Yale"; Darkhorse's own listing credits "Yale and American Academy of Arts & Sciences", and the tool site credits Yale ISPS APEX with the Academy. Confirmed. But I could not find any Darkhorse case study on CORE Score design beyond the award post.
- Darkhorse's current homepage and portfolio on darkhorseanalytics.com redirect or render client-side; I did not get their talks or conference write-ups (Information+, OpenVis, Malofiej). No search hit linked Darkhorse to those venues.
- I could not open the live CVI map, GCI (capitalismindex.com), AIDSVu or Cancer Prevention Community Profiles to inspect their per-place pages directly; descriptions above come from Darkhorse's and the tools' own text pages.

## Key question 2: How do comparable public tools structure a per-place profile or brief, and in what order?

### Takeaway
The consistent order across County Health Rankings, SDG Index, OECD Regional Well-being and CVI is: headline position (rank, score or group) with an explicit comparison frame; then a per-dimension dashboard with a status colour and a trend symbol; then an indicator table with value, year, comparison values and source; then links to strategies or methodology. Narrative, where present, is short and templated; peer comparison is built on either a similarity match (OECD) or a banding into groups (CHR health groups) rather than raw rank alone.

### Cited Findings
- County Health Rankings and Roadmaps 2025 snapshot: two summary graphics, "Population Health and Well-being" and "Community Conditions" (older releases: Health Outcomes and Health Factors); each county is a dot on a continuum from least healthy to healthiest in the nation; counties are placed in Health Groups that "support data-informed comparisons" but "do not necessarily represent statistically significant differences". Each measure shows county, state and national values, definitions, methods and limitations; some measures are disaggregated for racialized groups. A snapshot toggle highlights "Areas to Explore" and areas of strength (described in an earlier release's blog, so the current UI may differ). [KHI 2025 CHRR resource guide](https://www.khi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025-County-Health-Rankings-Roadmaps-Resource-Guide.pdf); [CHRR FAQ](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/explore-health-rankings/faq-page); [CHRR How to Use Your Health Snapshot](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/how-to-use-your-county-health-snapshot) (page returned 403 to the fetcher; structure taken from search snippets and the KHI guide)
- SDG Index country profile (dashboards.sdgindex.org, 2026 edition): header with country and region; tabs "Overview", "Indicators", "Fact Sheet"; headline tiles "SDG Index Rank", "SDG Index Score", "Spillover Score"; a 17-goal grid with the prompt "Click on a goal to view more information"; legends for dashboard status from "SDG achieved" to "Information unavailable" and trend status from "On track or maintaining SDG achievement" to "Trend information unavailable"; a chart "Status of SDG targets for [country] (% trend indicators)"; a footer citation to the report. The Indicators tab is a list grouped by goal with the instruction "Click on an indicator for values, time series, and metadata"; source citation is a single global footer, not per indicator on the list page. [SDG Index Togo profile](https://dashboards.sdgindex.org/profiles/togo/); [Togo indicators](https://dashboards.sdgindex.org/profiles/togo/indicators)
- SDG Index methodology: scores "can be interpreted as a percentage of optimal performance"; trends are computed per indicator from annual growth since 2015 extrapolated to 2030, then combined at goal level; "SDG Index rankings and scores from one edition cannot be compared with the results from previous editions"; dashboards are presented for all UN member states including countries not in the Index, and country profiles "indicate any gaps in a country's available SDG data". [SDG Index methodology](https://dashboards.sdgindex.org/chapters/methodology); [SDR 2026 Part 5](https://s3.amazonaws.com/sustainabledevelopment.report/2026/sustainable-development-report-2026-part-5.pdf)
- OECD Regional Well-being (updated Oct 2025 to 2024 or latest data; 467 regions, 11 topics): each topic scored 0 to 10 relative to other regions; for each topic the page shows the region's score, its position within its country and among all OECD regions; a section "Regions with similar well-being profiles" lists regions from other countries with a similar pattern, clickable; indicator values appear "in its original unit (percentage, dollars, etc.)" below the chart; the page shows no per-value year or source and no region-specific narrative sentences; missing data display not described. [OECD Regional Well-being, Brussels profile](https://oecdregionalwellbeing.org/BE1.html); [OECD Regional Well-being home](https://oecdregionalwellbeing.org/)
- OECD Regions and Cities at a Glance 2024 (launched 6 Dec 2024): about 100 indicators for more than 2,700 regions and 1,200 cities; it is a report, not a per-place profile tool. [OECD media advisory](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/media-advisories/2024/11/oecd-to-launch-regions-and-cities-at-a-glance-2024-on-friday-6-december.html)
- CVI report order (see Q1): score and rank, Top Drivers, Composition, Indicator Spotlight, Indicator Tree, with geographic context toggles and print or link export. [CVI tutorial](https://climatevulnerabilityindex.org/tutorials/step3)
- CORE Score profile order: map, time series with percentile band, demographic breakdowns, links to methodology and final report; no driver or action sections. [corescore.us](https://corescore.us/)
- Other municipal dashboards surfaced (2024 to 2026, not fetched in depth): a Bolivia open-source dashboard built on a Municipal Sustainable Development Index of 62 indicators across 15 SDGs; Statistics Canada's Municipal Financial and Socioeconomic Data Dashboard for individual municipalities; Japan's Digital Agency municipal dashboard of about 300 indicators in seven categories with regular updates; SAEON's South Africa SDG dashboard aimed at municipal planners. [StatCan dashboard](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/o1/en/plus/1487-new-product-municipal-financial-and-socioeconomic-data-dashboard); [Japan municipal dashboard](https://www.digital.go.jp/en/resources/japandashboard/economy-fiscal-living-municipality); [SAEON SDG dashboards](https://sarva.saeon.ac.za/sdgs/)
- UK LG Inform Plus "report writer includes dynamic text, alongside charts, maps and tables, drawing on regularly updated metrics"; councils such as Derby publish neighbourhood profiles produced by it. The LGA page does not say whether the text is template or model generated. [LGA, LG Inform Plus](https://www.local.gov.uk/our-support/research-and-data/lg-inform-data-benchmarking/lg-inform-plus); [Derby neighbourhood profiles](https://www.derby.gov.uk/council-and-democracy/statistics-census-information/neighbourhood-profiles)

### Inferences
- A defensible brief skeleton for LDT, assembled from the above: (1) headline: pillar scores with the comparison frame stated in the same line (national percentile band, province rank, peer group); (2) a three-pillar dashboard with status colour and trend symbol where time series exist, and an explicit "information unavailable" state; (3) "top drivers" per pillar computed from indicator deviations, in the CVI sense; (4) indicator table with value, year, source, national and provincial comparators, and a missing-data mark; (5) actions section (see Q3); (6) methodology and edition note.
- CHR's Health Groups are the right model for LDT rankings across 806 Nepal municipalities: banding avoids implying that rank 412 differs meaningfully from rank 420. The CHR caveat wording ("do not necessarily represent statistically significant differences") is directly reusable.
- OECD's "regions with similar well-being profiles" is the one peer pattern that does not require a predefined peer group; a nearest-neighbour match on the indicator vector would give each LDT municipality three to five comparators, and the brief could say what those peers do differently. Fit: good for all three countries; it also gives the plan-alignment and recommendations stages concrete comparators.
- SDG Index and OECD put the comparison-over-editions warning in methodology, not in the profile. For a tool used by World Bank staff who will compare years, LDT should put the edition warning in the brief itself.

### Gaps
- I could not open a live CHR county page, so the exact 2025 order of sections and the current wording of "Areas to Explore" are unverified.
- Our World in Data country profiles, UNDP HDR "Country Insights" pages, World Bank poverty or subnational briefs, and Opportunity Atlas tract pages were not retrievable (JavaScript or 403), and search returned only secondary summaries for UNDP. The secondary claim that HDI "values are not comparable across human development reports" because the whole series is recalculated annually is consistent with the SDG Index practice but is from an exam-prep site, so treat as unverified.
- No 2024 to 2026 World Bank subnational scorecard with per-municipality briefs surfaced in search beyond LDT's own domain.

## Key question 3: How do the best tools handle "what to do about it"?

### Takeaway
Only County Health Rankings' What Works for Health (WWFH) links a weak measure to a curated, rated catalogue of strategies; every other tool reviewed stops at "see methodology" or a static final report. WWFH's structure (strategies filed under the same factors as the measures, each with an evidence rating, a disparity rating, expected outcomes, implementation examples and citations) is the model for replacing free-text recommendations with a bounded catalogue.

### Cited Findings
- WWFH assigns each strategy an evidence rating "based on the quality, quantity, and findings of relevant research"; the scale in CHRR materials is Scientifically Supported, Some Evidence, Expert Opinion, Insufficient Evidence, Mixed Evidence, Evidence of Ineffectiveness. Separate disparity ratings describe how a strategy may affect racial, ethnic or socioeconomic gaps. [WWFH evidence and disparity ratings, Jan 2024](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/sites/default/files/media/document/What%20Works%20for%20Health%20Evidence%20and%20Disparity%20Ratings%2C%20Jan24.pdf); [WWFH overview 2025](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/sites/default/files/media/document/WWFH%20Overview%202025.pdf)
- CHRR guidance on using ratings: "the stronger the evidence of effectiveness, the more certain you can feel that a strategy will produce expected benefits", and the weaker the evidence "the more important it is to evaluate the effects of that strategy". [Identifying Equitable Solutions with WWFH, slides](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/sites/default/files/media/document/Identifying%20Equitable%20Solutions%20with%20What%20Works%20for%20Health%20Presentation%20Slides.pdf)
- CHRR positions the two products as a pair: the Rankings give "a snapshot of your county's current health AND" WWFH gives "information on strategies that can make your community healthier". [WWFH shortcut 2025](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/sites/default/files/media/document/WWFH%20Shortcut%202025.pdf); [Finding strategies in WWFH](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/strategies-and-solutions/what-works-for-health/finding-strategies-in-what-works-for-health) (page 403 to the fetcher)
- CVI: stated purpose is to show where "targeted benefits and resources should be directed", and the tutorial says tracts are "more relevant for frontline advocacy work", but it "does not give guidance on specific actions". [CVI methodology](https://climatevulnerabilityindex.org/methodology/); [CVI tutorial](https://climatevulnerabilityindex.org/tutorials/step3)
- CORE Score: action is delegated to the Commission's static "Final Report" ("American economy recommendations people first"); the profile has no action section. [corescore.us](https://corescore.us/)
- Opportunity Atlas: authors explicitly caution against the obvious action ("not necessarily that moving is the best solution"). [Opportunity Insights atlas summary](https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/atlas_summary.pdf)
- SDG Index: profiles end at indicator metadata and a report citation; no strategy layer. [Togo profile](https://dashboards.sdgindex.org/profiles/togo/)

### Inferences
- The WWFH design decision that matters for LDT is that strategies are indexed by the same taxonomy as the measures, so "weak indicator X" resolves to "strategies filed under factor X" by lookup, not by generation. For LDT this means building a per-pillar, per-indicator strategy catalogue (drawn from the national plan PDFs the plan-alignment stage already ingests, World Bank operational knowledge, and country sector strategies) and having the investment-recommendations stage select and rank from it, citing the catalogue entry.
- WWFH's evidence rating vocabulary would transfer with modest relabeling (for example "Evidence from the country's own plan", "Evidence from comparable LMIC programmes", "Expert opinion"). A disparity rating has a natural LDT analogue in "who benefits" (rural wards, women, marginalised groups) given Nepal's and Zambia's equity framing.
- WWFH's "evaluate it if the evidence is weak" guidance maps to an LDT recommendation field "how to know it worked", which also gives planners a monitoring hook.
- Nothing in the reviewed tools generates free-text recommendations; the tools that reach action do so through a curated catalogue or a human-authored report. That is the strongest argument in these notes against an unconstrained SWOT and recommendations stage.

### Gaps
- Could not open a WWFH strategy page, so the exact field list (expected beneficial outcomes, implementation examples, resources, citations) is from CHRR overview PDFs and search snippets rather than a live page.
- No example found of an LMIC subnational tool with a strategy catalogue; the LDT would be building that layer rather than copying one.

## Key question 4: How do they show provenance, data vintage, uncertainty and missing data inside a place profile?

### Takeaway
The strongest practices are explicit visual states for "no data" (SDG Index's "Information unavailable" and "Trend information unavailable" categories), documented imputation rules with thresholds (CVI), a sentinel plus exclusion rule (SVI's -999), banding to avoid false precision (CHR health groups), and per-measure definition, method and limitation text (CHR). Per-value year and source inside the profile is rarer than one would expect: OECD and the SDG Index list page show neither at the value level.

### Cited Findings
- SDG Index: dashboard legend includes "Information unavailable" as a status category and "Trend information unavailable" as a trend category; profiles "indicate any gaps in a country's available SDG data"; editions are not comparable; per-indicator "values, time series, and metadata" are one click deeper. [Togo profile](https://dashboards.sdgindex.org/profiles/togo/); [SDG methodology](https://dashboards.sdgindex.org/chapters/methodology)
- CVI: imputation by median of the next larger geography; cap of 25 percent imputed per indicator; county or state values copied to every tract where finer data do not exist; Alaska and Hawaii left missing on nine datasets; data mostly 2017 to 2019; 2010 boundaries; limitations section names pre-pandemic data, uniform county-level values across tracts, and imputation uncertainty. [CVI methodology](https://climatevulnerabilityindex.org/methodology/)
- CDC/ATSDR SVI (documentation lineage 2014 to 2022): "-999" represents null or no data; cells with -999 "were not used for further calculations"; tracts with zero population have all percentile fields set to -999 and are added back after ranking; percentile ranks run 0 to 1; CDC cautions "against comparing the different versions of our databases". The 2014 and 2018 documents are pre-2023 but the convention persists in the 2022 documentation. [SVI 2014 documentation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/media/pdfs/2024/08/SVI2014Documentation_01192022_1.pdf); [SVI 2022 documentation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/media/pdfs/2024/10/SVI2022Documentation.pdf); [SVI data and documentation download](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/php/svi/svi-data-documentation-download.html)
- CHR: Health Groups "do not necessarily represent statistically significant differences"; each measure carries definitions, methods and limitations; citation format is specified ("University of Wisconsin Population Health Institute. County Health Rankings & Roadmaps 2025"). [KHI 2025 resource guide](https://www.khi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025-County-Health-Rankings-Roadmaps-Resource-Guide.pdf); [CHRR 2024 technical document](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/sites/default/files/media/document/2024%20CHRR%20Technical%20Document_1.pdf)
- CORE Score: shows the 5th to 95th percentile band of all counties on the time series; in the fetched state all values read "Data is unavailable" with no explanation. [corescore.us](https://corescore.us/)
- OECD Regional Well-being: values in original units beneath each chart; site-level note "updated to 2024 (or latest available year)"; no per-value year or source on the profile page; downloadable data file. [OECD Regional Well-being, Brussels](https://oecdregionalwellbeing.org/BE1.html)
- Opportunity Atlas: cohort years and single-tract estimation stated as interpretation limits. [Opportunity Insights atlas summary](https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/atlas_summary.pdf)
- Google Places API generative summaries carry a mandatory disclosure string ("Summarized with Gemini") and a link to flag a problem with the summary, exposed as fields alongside the text. [GMSPlaceGenerativeSummary reference](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/ios-sdk/reference/objc/Classes/GMSPlaceGenerativeSummary)

### Inferences
- LDT should adopt a tri-state per indicator and per pillar: value with year and source; imputed or inherited from a higher tier (CVI's "county value copied to every tract" is exactly what happens when a Nepal district-level indicator is applied to its municipalities); and unavailable, excluded from the score (SVI's -999 rule). The brief should state how many of a pillar's indicators fell in each state before narrating the pillar.
- Because LDT's indicator vintages differ by country (Nepal 2021 census era, Serbia and Zambia different survey years), the per-value year belongs in the brief's indicator table, not only in the methodology page; OECD's omission of it is the pattern to avoid.
- The evidence-gap badge LDT already has is the equivalent of the SDG "Information unavailable" state; the brief should carry it through into each stage so a SWOT weakness is never built on an unavailable indicator.
- Banding (CHR) is the honest way to express rank among 806 units; the brief can say "upper third nationally, mid group in Province 3" rather than "rank 287".
- Google's generative-summary disclosure plus "report a problem" link is a minimal, shipped precedent for labeling generated text and giving readers a correction path.

### Gaps
- No tool reviewed showed confidence intervals or margins of error inside the place profile itself; CHR provides them in downloads (per earlier documentation) but I could not verify the 2025 UI.
- Could not confirm how the SVI interactive map renders -999 tracts.

## Key question 5: Are there examples of generated narrative text accompanying a place profile, and what are the lessons or criticisms?

### Takeaway
Among the tools reviewed, none ships LLM-written narrative inside a place profile; the only generated text found is templated "dynamic text" in LG Inform Plus reports and Gemini summaries in Google Places (a consumer, not policy, product). Official-statistics regulators and the OECD frame the risk precisely: AI summaries hide how figures were selected, interpreted and simplified, and detach numbers from source and revisions; LocalBench finds LLMs reach only 56.8 percent on county-level narrative questions, so a brief must be grounded in the tool's own data rather than model memory.

### Cited Findings
- Darkhorse, CVI, CORE Score, GCI, SDG Index, OECD Regional Well-being: no AI or generated narrative mentioned on any page fetched (see Q1 and Q2 sources). [Darkhorse Solutions](https://darkhorsevisualization.com/solutions); [CVI tutorial](https://climatevulnerabilityindex.org/tutorials/step3); [Togo profile](https://dashboards.sdgindex.org/profiles/togo/); [OECD Regional Well-being](https://oecdregionalwellbeing.org/BE1.html)
- LG Inform Plus (UK Local Government Association): report writer "includes dynamic text, alongside charts, maps and tables"; reports combine text, maps, charts and tables and can be exported. Not stated whether text is template or model generated. [LGA LG Inform Plus](https://www.local.gov.uk/our-support/research-and-data/lg-inform-data-benchmarking/lg-inform-plus)
- Google Places generative summaries: disclosure text "Summarized with Gemini" is delivered with the summary together with a link where users can flag a problem. [Google Places iOS SDK reference](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/ios-sdk/reference/objc/Classes/GMSPlaceGenerativeSummary)
- Google Data Commons Gemini extension (July 2024 coverage): results "come straight from Data Commons, including a link to the original data source", so the figures are retrieved rather than generated. [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/07/15/gemini-extensions-data-commons/)
- OECD blog "Protecting data quality in the AI era of official statistics" (July 2026): asks what happens to trust when AI "sits between institutions and the public" and says the answer is not yet clear; accessible answers could build trust while "detaching figures from their source" could erode it; AI systems can miss revisions or withdrawals. Secondary coverage phrases the warning as AI summaries that "obscure how information has been selected, interpreted and simplified". ### Inferences
- The field's working consensus (Data Commons retrieval, StatCan retrieval chatbot, ONS "AI-ready" attribution, OECD source-detachment warning) is that generated text may explain numbers but must not originate them, and every figure must link back to its source record. For the LDT brief: every numeric claim in the narrative should be a rendered token from the municipality's data, with the indicator id, year and source attached, and the model should not be allowed to introduce numbers that are not in its context.
- LocalBench's 56.8 percent and the "generic or stereotyped" finding are the specific risk for LDT's optional web-context stage and for any municipality the model has never seen (most of 806 Nepal municipalities). Treat web context as cited quotations with URLs, never as model recollection, and consider dropping it for municipalities where the search yields nothing municipality-specific.
- Labeling precedent: Google's "Summarized with Gemini" plus a flag link; OSR's expectation of trustworthiness, quality and value. The LDT brief should carry a persistent generated-text label, the model and prompt version, the data edition, and a "report a problem" path.
- The absence of LLM narrative in Darkhorse and the public index tools is itself a finding: these publishers chose drillable structure and templated status language (SDG arrows, CHR groups) over prose. A hybrid for LDT is to make stages one and two (evidence narrative, plan alignment) structured and templated with model-written sentences only for the connective tissue, and to make the SWOT and recommendations select from a catalogue (Q3).
