# Grounding plan analysis in policy documents with verifiable citations

Scope: how AI tools ingest development plans, extract commitments, map them to goals or indicators, cite pages, and turn analysis into recommendations without inventing projects.
Context is the LDT planning brief for municipalities in Nepal, Serbia and Zambia.
Research date: 29 October 2026.
Material older than 2024 is labelled with its year.

## Key question 1: Plan and SDG alignment tools (RIA, SDG Mapper, OSDG, SDG Synergies, VLR tools, World Bank, others)

### Takeaway
Every mature alignment tool separates three things the LDT currently merges into one LLM call: a document-to-goal classifier (keyword or small ML model, with humans reviewing the vocabulary), a coverage view (which goals or targets the plan addresses, how prominently, and where it is off-track), and a deliberately human step for priorities and interactions.
None of them lets a generative model decide alignment unsupervised; LLMs appear only as keyword suggesters (JRC 2025) or as a fine-tuned, database-constrained assistant (World Bank ImpactAI).

### Cited Findings
- UNDP's Rapid Integrated Assessment (RIA) reviews national plans and sector strategies against SDG targets to assess readiness and mainstream the goals into national and subnational planning - [NDC Partnership RIA entry](https://ndcpartnership.org/knowledge-portal/climate-toolbox/rapid-integrated-assessment-ria-tool-facilitate-mainstreaming-sdgs-national-and-local-plans)
- The AI-assisted RIA (2018, older) used paragraph embeddings to find passages in national plans that "match the semantic concepts of each of the SDG targets", used past manual RIAs from other countries as "neighborhood-based supervision", was tested on Bhutan, Cambodia, Liberia, Mauritius and Namibia, piloted on Papua New Guinea, and UNDP estimated it would cut assessment time from 3-4 weeks to 3 days; no precision or recall figures are given in the abstract - [Galsurkar et al., AAAI 2018](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11424)
- UNDP's current Integrated SDG Insights "National Priorities" module uses a bag-of-words shallow neural network chosen "for an increased interpretability and transparency", trained on the OSDG Community Dataset 2022.07 augmented with dictionary heuristics; it reached macro-average precision of 90% but recall of only 75% on the test set, with particularly poor recall for SDG 17 - [UNDP Methodological Note v1.4, 2023](https://data.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke476/files/2023-11/Methodology.pdf)
- The same note states its core assumption openly: "the amount of text linked to an SDG correlates with how important that SDG is", normalises counts by the highest count into a 0-1 "relative salience", and assigns tiers: high (> 0.75), medium (0.25 to 0.75), low (< 0.25) - [UNDP Methodological Note v1.4](https://data.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke476/files/2023-11/Methodology.pdf)
- Its alignment output is a two-axis overlay: priority tier from the text analysis on one axis and SDG trend status on the other, so users "see which SDGs might be low priority but off-track and vice-versa" - [UNDP Methodological Note v1.4](https://data.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke476/files/2023-11/Methodology.pdf)
- Governments and UNDP country teams supply "a maximum of ten policy documents" (national development plans, budgets, SDG roadmaps), the VNR is the default input, and the note recommends making sure documents "cover different aspects of the country's policies" because thematic-only inputs skew the result - [UNDP Methodological Note v1.4](https://data.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke476/files/2023-11/Methodology.pdf)
- UNDP handles non-English documents by machine translation into English and tested robustness on 14 reports across 5 UN languages plus Portuguese and German: 91% of assigned priority tiers did not change under translation; listed limitations are poor SDG 17 performance, no native multilingual handling, and the salience assumption not always holding - [UNDP Methodological Note v1.4](https://data.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke476/files/2023-11/Methodology.pdf)
- The EC JRC SDG policy mapping is keyword-based: text is lowercased, stop words removed, and targets identified by designed keywords with handling for plural and word variants; in 2025 LLMs were used only to suggest keywords, growing the list from 3,400 to 4,903 entries, followed by expert review and a test on more than 50,000 documents to confirm detection improved "without inflating SDG counts" - [JRC KnowSDGs methodology](https://know-sdgs.jrc.ec.europa.eu/methodology-policy-mapping)
- JRC outputs are an interactive goal/target/policy drill-down plus network analysis of policies sharing targets; earlier mappings (2017) were done by expert groups and approved with each Commission department - [JRC KnowSDGs methodology](https://know-sdgs.jrc.ec.europa.eu/methodology-policy-mapping)
- The SDG Mapper API accepts text in any official EU language and returns detected SDGs, targets and indicators as JSON; API access requires a request to the JRC team - [JRC SDG Mapper](https://know-sdgs.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sdgmapper)
- A JRC/AFD comparison of the SDG Mapper and AFD's SDG Prospector on EU policy documents and World Bank material notes that results across tools can diverge - [JRC130904 report](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC130904)
- OSDG 2.0 supports content in 15 languages and relies on volunteer validation in which each text is shown with one suggested SDG label that volunteers accept or reject; the community dataset records accept and reject counts per text and is published quarterly on Zenodo, with the stated limitation that "all texts are validated against only one associated SDG label" - [OSDG 2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11252); [OSDG Community Dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/11441197)
- SEI's SDG Synergies tool builds a cross-impact matrix of target interactions through a "participatory, discussion-based scoring process" on a 7-point scale, with experts recording explanation and certainty per score, then applies network analysis; SEI warns that completing the matrix "can be a tedious task but there is no short-cut and the result is only as robust as the information in the matrix" - [SEI SDG Synergies](https://www.sei.org/tools/sdg-synergies/); [SEI factsheet](https://www.sei.org/publications/sdg-synergies-factsheet/)
- UN DESA's VLR guidelines provide a checklist of issues a local review could reflect; UCLG and UN-Habitat's VLR series Volume 2 covers making VLRs and VNRs compatible; the European Handbook for SDG Voluntary Local Reviews (2022 edition) gives consolidated indicator examples for local and regional governments - [UN DESA VLR guidelines](https://hlpf.un.org/tools/guidelines-for-voluntary-local-reviews); [UCLG/UN-Habitat VLR Vol 2](https://www.old.uclg.org/sites/default/files/210718_vlrguidelines_vol2.pdf); [European Handbook](https://sustainabledevelopment.un.org/content/documents/26789European_Handbook_for_SDG_Voluntary_Local_Reviews.pdf)
- UN-Habitat's Asia-Pacific regional VLR guidelines include a section on linking local priorities to plans, and UN-Habitat has been tasked by the UN General Assembly to lead a harmonised set of local SDG and New Urban Agenda indicators - [UN-Habitat Asia-Pacific VLR guidelines](https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/2021/07/asia-pacific_regional_guidelines_on_vlrs_0.pdf)
- World Bank DIME's ImpactAI is built on a curated base of 15,000+ randomized trials across 150+ countries, "puts every effect on one scale and traces each back to its source", answers what works, compares program effectiveness, "flags where evidence is thin, and suggests alternatives"; the Bank reports extraction accuracy of about 85%, "on par with human experts"; it debuted at the Google.org Impact Summit in September 2024 and had its public launch at the October 2025 Annual Meetings - [World Bank DIME AI Lab](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/unit/unit-dec/impactevaluation/ai); [Science and AI for Development Impact event, Oct 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/events/2025/10/17/science-ai-for-development-impact)
- ImpactAI's scope is development economics research only; the FAQ describes a fine-tuned LLM producing answers "strictly constrained to the verified database" (the FAQ page returned 404 on fetch, so this wording comes from the search snippet) - [ImpactAI FAQ (snippet only)](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/unit/unit-dec/impactevaluation/ai/impact-ai/faq)

### Inferences
- The RIA and UNDP Insights pattern is "classify passages to targets, aggregate into coverage, overlay with indicator status" and this is exactly the shape a municipal plan alignment view should take: which LDT indicators or domains the plan addresses, how prominently, and where the plan is silent but the score is weak.
- Every tool keeps the vocabulary (keywords, labels, matrix scores) human-reviewed and versioned; LLM output is treated as a draft classifier or a keyword suggester, not the final map.
- Interactions and priorities (SDG Synergies) are deliberately participatory; an LLM should not score trade-offs between a municipality's goals on its own.
- UNDP's 91% tier stability under machine translation suggests translate-then-classify is acceptable for coarse coverage tiers, but not evidence that page-level quotes survive translation.

### Gaps
- No public technical description of ImpactAI's citation mechanism, base model, or how "85% extraction accuracy" was measured; only program pages were found.
- No 2025-2026 World Bank public tool for aligning subnational plans with indicators was found; Documents and Reports search was not covered in results.
- No IMF or OECD equivalent for plan-to-goal alignment surfaced in searches.
- The full RIA AAAI paper (precision/recall, how experts validated matches) was not retrieved; only the abstract.

## Key question 2: Government AI document-analysis tools with citations (i.AI, US federal, Singapore Pair, others)

### Takeaway
Published government evaluations show the same design everywhere: a two-stage pipeline with a human review step at each stage, accuracy measured as agreement with expert reviewers (F1 around 0.76, with reviewers themselves disagreeing with each other 40% of the time), and the honest finding that the tool's weakest point is missing themes rather than wrong ones.
Citations are offered at the chunk level, but no government has published a citation-accuracy evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- i.AI's Consult works in two stages, theme generation and theme mapping, "with a human in the loop" at each: four Scottish Government experts reviewed generated themes and six reviewed mapped themes through an interface showing each response with its themes - [Consult evaluation report, Scottish Government NSCP consultation](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Scot_Gov_NSCP_Evaluation_Report.pdf)
- Results: multi-label F1 of 0.76 against reviewer labels, reviewers made no changes for 60% of responses, median review time 23 seconds per response with 77% reviewed in under a minute, and theme rankings ("the key driver of policy recommendations") were near-identical - [Consult evaluation report](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Scot_Gov_NSCP_Evaluation_Report.pdf)
- The report cautions that "there is nothing saying the reviewer label is 'correct'": in a previous unpublished evaluation two human reviewers disagreed on exact mappings 40% of the time and mapped the exact same themes only 62% of the time - [Consult evaluation report](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Scot_Gov_NSCP_Evaluation_Report.pdf)
- Failure modes: "Consult struggled to identify missing themes"; reviewers added an "Other" theme for 14% of responses; reviewers used "No Reason Given" nearly 17 times more than the tool; changes concentrated on a few difficult themes; reviewers wanted to review only a sample of responses "but needed more confidence in the mapping before doing that" - [Consult evaluation report](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Scot_Gov_NSCP_Evaluation_Report.pdf)
- The UK government said further testing and evaluation of Consult will happen before any wider rollout decision - [gov.uk press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-built-humphrey-ai-tool-reviews-responses-to-consultation-for-first-time-in-bid-to-save-millions)
- The Department for Transport's Consultation Analysis Tool (built with the Alan Turing Institute) was evaluated in a blind comparison against human-analysed datasets and in a live pilot where humans amended initial output; the evaluation also tested whether accuracy was systematically lower for protected characteristics as a bias proxy - [DfT CAT evaluation](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-consultation-analysis-tool-evaluation)
- Redbox is described by i.AI as RAG-based chat with "granular citations"; the only independent test found (Global Government Forum, July 2024) rated quality "generally high" but observed Redbox drew on only six of ten transcripts for its first answer; i.AI said the model had since improved - [GGF Digital Leaders Study 2024](https://digital.globalgovernmentforum.com/digital-leaders-study-24-2/digital-leaders-study-2-finding3-2-2/)
- The ai.gov.uk Consult page was reachable only through a UK web archive copy at the time of research - [Consult page (archived)](https://tnaqa.mirrorweb.com/ukgwa/20260902044439mp_/https://ai.gov.uk/knowledge-hub/tools/consult)
- Singapore's Pair was framed in a 2023 parliamentary reply as assistive: civil servants "are still directly responsible for making policy decisions, as well as crafting, refining, and customising the content", and work with highly confidential or sensitive information "will still be written exclusively by civil servants" - [MDDI parliamentary reply, 2023](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/28022023/)
- GovTech states Pair is used by tens of thousands of civil servants; no published Pair hallucination or citation evaluation was found - [GovTech AI and data page](https://www.tech.gov.sg/about-us/what-we-do/govtech-role-in-digital-transformation-for-the-public-sector/ai-and-data-in-singapore-government/)
- GSA launched USAi in August 2025 as a "secure generative artificial intelligence evaluation suite" at no cost to agencies, with chat, code generation and document summarisation, and a console to compare models using standardised evaluation frameworks and custom evaluation tests - [GSA press release, Aug 2025](https://www.gsa.gov/about-us/newsroom/news-releases/gsa-launches-usai-to-advance-white-house-americas-ai-action-plan-08142025)
- Argentina's public administration prompting guide (OECD.AI Policy Navigator) treats AI outputs as preliminary inputs with mandatory human supervision - [OECD.AI Policy Navigator entry](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/policy-initiatives/prompting-guide-for-public-administration)
- On neutrality: a September 2025 study reported by MLex concluded perfect political neutrality in LLMs is impossible but can be approximated, with closed models (ChatGPT, Claude) avoiding biased responses better than two open models - [MLex, Sept 2025](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2387680)
- A May 2025 study using Germany's Wahl-O-Mat found a lean toward left-leaning parties, strongest in larger models, and that the language used to query a model shifted its political views - [arXiv 2505.04393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04393v1)

### Inferences
- The Consult numbers set a realistic ceiling: an LLM mapping plan commitments to LDT indicators will probably agree with an expert around three-quarters of the time, and experts will disagree among themselves almost as often, so the brief should present mappings as reviewable drafts and show confidence rather than claim correctness.
- "Missing themes" is the public-sector failure mode to design for: the brief needs an explicit "plan is silent on X" output, not only "plan covers X".
- Pair's rule that sensitive content stays human-written argues for keeping the LLM's role in the brief descriptive (what the plan says, where) and letting humans write the politically loaded judgement.
- The finding that query language shifts model political stance matters directly for Nepali and Serbian prompts; the same plan analysed in two languages may come out with different emphasis.

### Gaps
- No government has published a citation-accuracy evaluation (span correctness, fabricated citations) for Redbox, Consult, Minute, Extract or Pair.
- i.AI Extract and Minute evaluations were not found in searches.
- No formal OECD or UN document on political neutrality for generative AI advising governments was found; only general human-oversight guidance.

## Key question 3: Citation and grounding techniques that hold up (page or span citations, quote-then-claim, extract-then-analyse, quote verification; long, scanned, non-English PDFs)

### Takeaway
The reliable stack is: extract text with a layout-aware OCR into page-indexed Markdown, make the model return a verbatim quote plus page for every claim, and verify each quote by exact or fuzzy string match before showing it.
Native citation APIs do this verification for you (Anthropic returns guaranteed-valid pointers with 1-indexed pages) but only on PDFs with extractable text; scanned Nepali and Serbian documents must go through OCR first, and OCR is where the unrecoverable errors happen.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic's Citations feature chunks PDFs and plain text into sentences, returns `page_location` citations with 1-indexed start pages and exclusive end pages, `char_location` for text, and `content_block_location` for custom content documents where "no further chunking is done" - [Anthropic Citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)
- Anthropic states citations "are guaranteed to contain valid pointers to the provided documents" because the API parses them and extracts `cited_text` directly, and that in its own evaluations the feature "is significantly more likely to cite the most relevant quotes" than prompt-based approaches; `cited_text` does not count toward output tokens and citations work with prompt caching and batch processing - [Anthropic Citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)
- Limit relevant to LDT: "PDFs that are scans of documents and do not contain extractable text are not citable" and image citations are not supported - [Anthropic Citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)
- Anthropic's prompting guidance for inputs over 20k tokens: put long documents at the top, wrap each in `<document>` tags with `<source>` metadata, and "ask Claude to quote relevant parts of the documents first before carrying out its task" - [Anthropic prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- OpenAI file search returns `file_citation` annotations carrying `file_id`, `filename` and `index`; retrieved chunks are only returned when `include=["file_search_call.results"]` is set - [OpenAI file search guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-file-search)
- Developers report that under the Responses API "there's no longer any way to determine which specific chunk was referenced when a citation is produced" - [OpenAI community thread](https://community.openai.com/t/file-search-annotations-reference-a-file-rather-than-a-chunk/1367438)
- Google Vertex AI RAG Engine returns `grounding_supports` linking a generated text segment (with character start and end offsets) to `grounding_chunk_indices` and per-chunk confidence scores, with chunks carrying `uri` and `title` - [Vertex AI RAG output reference](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/model-reference/rag-output-explained?hl=en)
- The "Traceable by Design" EU consultation pipeline (2026) requires every extracted topic to carry "at least one evidence quote copied exactly from the passage, with no paraphrasing, no shortening, no normalisation, and no correction", enforces this with a Pydantic schema, and verifies quotes against source paragraphs by exact and fuzzy string matching with a 99.1% match rate; the remaining mismatches are flagged as "potential model hallucinations that should be further analysed" - [Bertaglia et al., arXiv 2605.30995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30995)
- The same paper's OCR stage uses the Mistral OCR API producing per-page Markdown plus JSON; cleaning drops boilerplate lines appearing on more than 60% of pages, flags pages with unique-to-total word ratio below 0.15 as garbled, removes table-of-contents pages and pages under 30 words, and buckets documents losing more than two pages into a `flag/` directory for review - [Bertaglia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30995)
- Its authors conclude "OCR and chunking are the real bottlenecks": "No prompt refinement recovers a position paper whose columns were read across rather than down"; chunk scoring "degrades on tables, itemised policy recommendations, and short formulaic statements"; they recommend manual sampling of documents before a full run, retaining intermediate outputs at every stage, and versioning the prompt with the code - [Bertaglia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30995)
- Their negative rules (extract only what is explicitly supported, return empty output for names, headers and procedural text) are hypothesised to cause a 33.6% empty-output rate, and independent per-paragraph extraction produced 1,458 emergent labels consolidated to 224 - [Bertaglia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30995)
- ALCE (2023, older) found that even the best models lacked full citation support about half the time on ELI5; its automatic citation recall metric agreed with humans 85.1% of the time and citation precision 77.6%, and the NLI checker "can't detect partial support" - [Gao et al., EMNLP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627v2)
- LongCite (Findings of ACL 2025) introduced LongBench-Cite for sentence-level citations in long-context QA, reported current LLMs have "considerable room to improve", and found that fine-tuning with citation data also improved answer correctness - [Zhang et al., arXiv 2409.02897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02897v2)
- On scanned Nepali documents (600 government and NGO notices, Google OCR), a fine-tuned layout model (LiLT) reached F1 87.65 while GPT-4o's exact-match accuracy was 0.55 for Date, 0.25 for Subject and 0.66 for Signed By (0.56, 0.81 and 0.85 at 90% match) and Llama 3.1 8B was worse; the authors note LLMs have "difficulty in localizing the extracted tokens back to their exact positions in the original document" - [CHiPSAL 2025 Nepali extraction paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.chipsal-1.13)
- A 2026 Devanagari OCR stress test of ten systems found clean synthetic text looked similar across systems but real scans caused most to degrade sharply, reporting chrF++ on real scans of 86.3 (Gemini), 82.2 (Claude) and 58.5 (GPT-5.5), with DeepSeek-OCR showing rare catastrophic repetition failures; single preprint, model names unverified - [arXiv 2606.29213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29213)
- For Nepali PDFs (2024), PDF text parsers were faster but their accuracy "fluctuates based on PDF types", while Tesseract OCR gave consistent accuracy at longer runtime - [arXiv 2407.04577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04577v2)
- For Serbian Cyrillic, a Tesseract-based OCR on dialect dictionaries reached 97.19% to 99.87% accuracy (2022, older, different domain), and an OECD.AI entry on Serbia's legislation digitisation reports "initial challenges with OCR accuracy for Serbian Cyrillic, poor scan quality, and complex legal formatting" - [SCyDia, EURALEX 2022](https://euralex.org/publications/scydia-ocr-for-serbian-cyrillic-with-diacritics/); [OECD.AI Serbia entry](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/policy-initiatives/digiteks-application-of-ai-for-understanding-the-serbian-language-and-digitalization-in-the-field-of-legislation)
- Multilingual RAG research: query translation "suffers from limited coverage" while multilingual retrieval "introduces inconsistencies due to cross-language effects"; retrievers prefer high-resource and query languages and generators favour "query languages and Latin script languages" - [arXiv 2504.03616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03616v1); [arXiv 2502.11175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11175)
- UNDP's machine-translate-then-classify approach kept 91% of SDG priority tiers unchanged across 14 bilingual reports - [UNDP Methodological Note v1.4](https://data.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke476/files/2023-11/Methodology.pdf)

### Inferences
- For LDT, native citation APIs are usable for born-digital Serbian and English PDFs; for scanned Nepali plans the pipeline must OCR to page-tagged text first, then either feed pages as custom content blocks (one block per page or paragraph, preserving page numbers) or use the quote-plus-fuzzy-match pattern.
- Quote verification by string match is cheap, deterministic and catches the residual hallucinations that schemas alone miss; it should run on every quote the brief displays, and unmatched quotes should be dropped or flagged rather than shown.
- Keep the quote in the source language and translate only for display; a translated quote cannot be string-matched against the source, and the Cyrillic bias findings mean retrieval over Serbian text should not rely on an English query alone.
- Page-level pointers are the honest granularity for scanned documents; sentence-level spans are only trustworthy where OCR quality has been checked.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy figure for Anthropic page citations on OCR-derived text or on Nepali or Serbian PDFs.
- No benchmark of OCR accuracy on Nepali or Serbian municipal planning documents specifically; figures above come from notices, dictionaries and legal texts.
- No study found that compares translate-then-cite against cite-in-source-language for page-level evidence.

## Key question 4: Pre-ingestion into a structured store versus per-request retrieval

### Takeaway
Practitioners who need traceability parse once into page-indexed structured records with intermediate outputs retained and prompts versioned, then analyse from that store; per-request full-document reading is defensible only for small, stable corpora with prompt caching, and even then position effects and cost argue against re-reading a 300-page plan on every button click.
Plan documents change on multi-year cycles, so freshness is not the constraint.

### Cited Findings
- The Traceable by Design pipeline writes intermediate outputs at every stage (OCR JSON and Markdown, cleaned pages, chunk JSON with quality labels, paragraph JSON, extraction records), surfaces "model name, schema" and run metadata in the dashboard, and recommends to "retain intermediate outputs at every stage, version the prompt alongside the code, and surface pipeline configuration directly in any interface that presents results" - [Bertaglia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30995)
- UNDP ingests a fixed set of up to ten government-supplied documents once per Integrated SDG Insights report, and JRC pulls EUR-Lex documents in batch and republishes the mapping (2025 update covered documents to July 2025) - [UNDP Methodological Note v1.4](https://data.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke476/files/2023-11/Methodology.pdf); [JRC KnowSDGs methodology](https://know-sdgs.jrc.ec.europa.eu/methodology-policy-mapping)
- Anthropic's citations work with prompt caching and batch processing, and enabling citations adds "a slight increase in input tokens" from chunking while `cited_text` is free of output-token cost - [Anthropic Citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)
- A practitioner summary of a June 2026 study (secondary, unverified) reports long-context prompting at about 73.1% correctness versus 65.4% for semantic RAG but at roughly 26 times the per-query token cost - [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/long-context-beats-rag-on-accuracyso-why-keep-retrieval)
- Redis's engineering blog reports accuracy drops of 10-20+ percentage points when relevant information sits in the middle of long contexts, and that caching discounts only apply when data is shared and changes rarely - [Redis blog](https://redis.io/blog/rag-vs-large-context-window-ai-apps/)
- Nepal's Local Government Operation Act 2017, section 24(1), obliges every municipality to prepare periodic, annual, sectoral and mid- and long-term plans; Helambu Rural Municipality's first periodic plan covers FY 2080/81 to 2084/85 (five years) - [RECOFTC periodic plan guideline](https://recoftc.org/sites/default/files/publications/resources/recoftc-0000377-0001-en.pdf); [Helambu periodic plan](https://www.helambumun.gov.np/sites/helambumun.gov.np/files/Periodic%20Plan%20Of%20Helambu%20Rural%20Municipality%202080-81%20_%202084-85%20Final%20English%20book%202080-6-25.pdf)
- The CHiPSAL authors note that LLM extraction over long OCR text "increases computational costs significantly, making these methods more difficult to scale or apply in high-volume situations" - [CHiPSAL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.chipsal-1.13)

### Inferences
- A per-municipality plan store (document, version, page count, OCR quality flag, extracted commitments with page and verbatim quote, mapped indicator, extraction run metadata) is the pattern every traceable tool converges on; the brief's SWOT and recommendation steps should read from that store, not re-read PDFs.
- Freshness can be handled by re-ingesting when a new plan version is uploaded (annual plans yearly, periodic plans every five years); a content hash plus plan period is enough.
- Per-request reading is still reasonable for a first prototype on short born-digital plans with caching, but it cannot give consistent page citations across runs and cannot be audited after the fact.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found comparing extraction-once against per-request reading on accuracy for policy documents; the cost and position-effect figures come from blogs and a secondary report.
- No practitioner report on how often municipal plans in Nepal, Serbia or Zambia are revised or re-published mid-cycle.

## Key question 5: Tying recommendations to evidence instead of inventing interventions

### Takeaway
Evidence clearinghouses refuse to generate recommendations at all; they publish rated strategies and leave the choice to the reader, and J-PAL's framework insists the first steps (diagnosing the local problem) must use local data and local knowledge, not studies from elsewhere.
A brief that recommends should therefore only choose from a curated, rated list and must show the local diagnosis and the plan's own commitments that the recommendation builds on.

### Cited Findings
- 3ie states its evidence gap maps "do not provide recommendations for policy and programming or guidelines for practice" but "provide links to resources that can inform policy or programme development" - [3ie evidence gap maps](https://3ieimpact.org/evidence-hub/evidence-gap-maps)
- County Health Rankings' What Works for Health rates nearly 400 strategies on an explicit scale (Scientifically Supported, Some Evidence, Expert Opinion, Insufficient Evidence, Mixed Evidence, Evidence of Ineffectiveness) with separate disparity ratings, and advises that "an evidence rating applies only to the outcomes studied" so users should confirm the outcomes matter locally - [WWFH evidence and disparity ratings, Jan 2024](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/sites/default/files/media/document/What%20Works%20for%20Health%20Evidence%20and%20Disparity%20Ratings%2C%20Jan24.pdf); [Finding strategies in WWFH](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/strategies-and-solutions/what-works-for-health/finding-strategies-in-what-works-for-health)
- J-PAL's generalizability framework breaks "will this program work here?" into steps following the program's theory; "the first steps require a local diagnosis of the problem, and need to be answered using local descriptive data as well as qualitative interviews and local institutional knowledge", later behavioural steps can draw on studies elsewhere, and implementation steps depend on local process monitoring - [J-PAL generalizability framework](https://povertyactionlab.org/node/4560)
- ImpactAI constrains answers to a curated base of causal evidence, compares effects on one scale traced to source, and "flags where evidence is thin" rather than filling the gap - [World Bank DIME AI Lab](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/unit/unit-dec/impactevaluation/ai)
- PDIA (2012, older) argues many reforms become "isomorphic mimicry" where institutions change how they look but not what they do, and its first principle is to focus on "problems that local actors define" rather than transplanting "best practice" solutions - [Andrews, Pritchett, Woolcock, CID WP 240](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/cid/publications/faculty-working-papers/escaping-capability-traps-through-problem-driven-iterative-adaptation-pdia)
- Nepal's periodic plan guideline requires that each local plan align with relevant higher-level policies (the text was truncated in the result so the exact list of policies is unconfirmed) - [RECOFTC periodic plan guideline](https://recoftc.org/sites/default/files/publications/resources/recoftc-0000377-0001-en.pdf)
- The Consult evaluation treats theme rankings as "the key driver of policy recommendations", keeping the recommendation itself with officials - [Consult evaluation report](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Scot_Gov_NSCP_Evaluation_Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- The safe recommendation design for LDT is a closed-vocabulary one: the model selects from a maintained intervention catalogue (with evidence rating, outcome studied, and source), cites the plan commitment it extends or the gap it fills, and never free-writes a project; unrated items should appear as "options to investigate" rather than recommendations.
- J-PAL's ordering maps onto the brief: step one is the LDT score diagnosis plus the plan's own statements (local), step two is evidence from elsewhere, step three is implementation notes the municipality must supply.
- For a World Bank public tool used by governments, the 3ie posture (inform, do not prescribe) is the defensible default; recommendations should be worded as options tied to the municipality's own stated goals, with the evidence rating visible.

### Gaps
- No documented example was found of an LLM tool that restricts investment recommendations to a rated catalogue in a subnational development context; ImpactAI is the closest but covers research synthesis, not municipal investment.
- No curated evidence catalogue for Nepal, Serbia or Zambia municipal interventions was found in this search.

## Key question 6: SWOT-style outputs, critiques and alternatives

### Takeaway
The published critique of SWOT is that its outputs are rarely used in later strategy steps and that it blurs categories and hides trade-offs; nothing found evaluates LLM-generated SWOTs.
The alignment tools reviewed use more specific structures instead: coverage-versus-status matrices, gap lists, rated options, and participatory interaction matrices.

### Cited Findings
- Hill and Westbrook (1997, older) reviewed 50 companies, found over 20 used SWOT, and reported that "no-one subsequently used the outputs within the later stages of the strategy process", concluding its continued use "needs to be questioned" - [Hill and Westbrook, Long Range Planning 30(1)](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:e44a6a9e-9537-4fb6-848c-2d20998a7ce6)
- An encyclopaedia entry summarises SWOT's weaknesses as "oversimplification of strategic factors, categorical ambiguities, and failure to address strategic tradeoffs" - [SAGE Encyclopedia of Management Theory entry](https://studysites.sagepub.com/gray3e/study/chapter3/Encyclopaedia%20entries/SWOT_Analysis_Framework.pdf)
- A 2006 (older) public-sector planning paper notes that recommendations to analyse and evaluate action-plan alternatives "aren't concretized to the level of quantitative analysis and evaluation of alternatives" - [Bivainis and Tunčikienė, Business: Theory and Practice](https://journals.vilniustech.lt/index.php/BTP/article/view/11632)
- UNDP's alternative structure is a priority-versus-trend overlay that surfaces "low priority but off-track" goals - [UNDP Methodological Note v1.4](https://data.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke476/files/2023-11/Methodology.pdf)
- SEI's SDG Synergies replaces internal/external framing with a scored interaction matrix between targets, produced participatorily - [SEI SDG Synergies](https://www.sei.org/tools/sdg-synergies/)
- J-PAL's framework replaces a generic strengths list with a local diagnosis step answered from local data and institutional knowledge - [J-PAL generalizability framework](https://povertyactionlab.org/node/4560)
- WWFH presents rated strategies with outcome caveats rather than opportunity lists - [WWFH ratings](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/sites/default/files/media/document/What%20Works%20for%20Health%20Evidence%20and%20Disparity%20Ratings%2C%20Jan24.pdf)
- The Consult evaluation found reviewers felt the tool "helped reduce reviewer bias" on theme generation, but that it "struggled to identify missing themes" - [Consult evaluation report](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Scot_Gov_NSCP_Evaluation_Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- For municipal officials, a SWOT generated from scores and plan text repeats what the score table and the alignment matrix already say, and the "threats" and "weaknesses" quadrants are the politically loaded parts most likely to read as prescription from a World Bank tool.
- A better brief structure, following the tools above: (1) what the plan commits to, with page quotes; (2) coverage matrix of plan commitments against LDT indicators, highlighting indicators that are weak and unaddressed; (3) rated options drawn from a catalogue, each tied to a commitment or a gap; (4) open questions for the municipality (local diagnosis the model cannot do).
- If a SWOT is kept for familiarity, each cell should be required to cite either a score or a page quote, and cells without evidence should be left empty rather than filled.

### Gaps
- No study was found that evaluates the quality or usefulness of LLM-generated SWOT analyses, in government or elsewhere.
- No guidance from the World Bank or UNDP specifically on the wording of AI-generated assessments of a government's own plans (neutrality, non-prescription) was found; the closest are the general human-oversight rules in Key question 2.
- Full text of Hill and Westbrook and of the Bivainis paper was not retrieved; findings rest on abstracts.
